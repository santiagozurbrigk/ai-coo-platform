# Trends Report

The Trends Report is a report that helps you identify patterns, fluctuations, and anomalies in your ad and sales data over a selected date range.

---

#### Generate a Trends report

In **Hyros**: **Reporting** → **Other Reports** → **Trends** → configure:

1. Attribution Model: pick the model that fits your analysis (e.g. Last Click)

2. Date range: the overall period to analyze (e.g. last 30 days)

3. Traffic source: filter to a specific platform via Specify Attributes (e.g. Google Ads)

4. Advanced Options:

- Show only first-time customers to focus on new acquisitions
- Exclude recurring sales so the data reflects fresh purchases only

---

#### Reading the report

The report shows your data broken down across multiple rolling time windows (e.g. last 3 days, last 7 days, last 30 days) for each metric. Compare values across columns to spot trends.

**Key columns to watch:**

| Column | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| **Unique Customers** | Are you acquiring more or fewer new customers recently? |
| **Cost** | Is ad spend stable, growing, or shrinking? |
| **Return on Ad Spend (ROAS)** | Are your ads more or less efficient compared to longer ranges? |
| **Cost to Acquire Customer (CAC)** | Is it getting cheaper or more expensive to win new customers? |

---

**Example:**

If your **30-day ROAS is lower than your 7-day or 3-day ROAS**, that's a signal your ads are converting faster in the short term than the long-term average.

**What this means:**

Your campaigns are currently performing above their baseline. Customers are converting quickly, which is exactly what you want.

**What to do:**

1. Identify what changed in the last 3-7 days — new creative, new audience, new campaign, new offer

2. Double down on those changes — increase budget, expand audiences, replicate the winning combination

3. Make sure all ads convert customers as fast as possible to keep the short-term efficiency high

If the trend reverses (longer-range ROAS is better than recent), that's a sign of declining ad efficiency — investigate before increasing spend.

Using CAC trends to scale smarter

💡 **Net CAC = the true cost of acquiring a customer.**

When Net CAC is trending down

You're acquiring customers more efficiently — a strong signal to scale.

When Net CAC is trending up

Your acquisition costs are rising — investigate before scaling further.
